Pencil Sketch of Lord Krishna

Significance of Krishna's flute : The flute, in Krishna's hands, symbolizes complete surrender and detachment. Just as the flute allows itself to be played by the musician, devotees are encouraged to surrender their individual will to the divine and let themselves be guided by the cosmic orchestrator. This surrender is a reminder that the ego and desires should not obstruct the divine flow of life.

Why I chose to paint Lotus OilPainting

This aquatic bloom thrives in muddy waters, emerging as a symbol of purity and enlightenment. it represents purity and tranquility. This resilience is a powerful reminder of how beauty can emerge from difficult circumstances.

Rukhmini and Krishnas Cross Stich

we can understand the eternal relationship between the divine couple Sri Rukmini Krishna. She is personification of his divine attributes. She is his ichha shakti “willing potency”. She is the canvas through which he projects his kalyana gunas. He express his desires through her. For Sristi, Sthiti, Laya, Anugraha and Nigraha, they play the game in which she desires and he fulfills. He especially projects some special kalyana gunas like compassion, forgiveness and extreme love towards the devotees only through her. Gopala Tapani upanisada says Rukmini is the eternal consort of Krishna who is never separable from him and she is the potency with which he creates, operates and destroys the entire creations.

Hibiscus in the Garden

These hibiscus blossoms in the Garden invite butterflies and hummingbirds to reach in for a deep drink of sweet nectar. As they do, they brush against the flower’s center and get a dusting of pollen. When they carry that pollen to another hibiscus flower, some of it falls off and fertilizes the flower.

Fun fact :
A hibiscus flower tucked behind the right ear tells everyone you are single. Behind the left ear, it means that you already have a partner. “Right means you’re looking; left means you’re taken.”
